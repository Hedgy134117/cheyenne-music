Great albums: February 2026

1. The Mountain, by Gorillaz - I’m still digesting this one since it’s only been out for around a week as of writing this, but I know for a fact that it is very good! On *The Mountain,* Damon Albarn steers his omnivorous musical interests in the direction of Indian classical music, and enlists talented musicians that include Anoushka Shankar, Asha Bhosle, and Asha Puthli (whose Indian pop/disco albums I highly recommend) to realize this sound for the album. Conceptually, the album focuses mostly on death, and even features posthumous contributions from previous Gorillaz collaborators like Dennis Hopper and Dave of De La Soul. Despite loving the singles “The Happy Dictator” and “Damascus,” I don’t think anything could have prepared me for how much I love this album - it is without a doubt their most well-executed creative vision since *Plastic Beach.*  
2. URGH, by Mandy, Indiana - on their sophomore project, the French electro-industrial duo really step it up, with beats that feel meaner and even more bass-boosted than their debut, and songs that feel significantly more fleshed out. The song structures are still very experimental (especially considering moments like the unexpected string outro in the glitched-out opener “Sevastopol”), but somehow pack in some unfathomable grooves - especially on lead single “Magazine.” Vocally, the band seem to be most influenced by hip hop, and bring in highly visceral energy as well as off-kilter, rhythmically surprising flows. From start to finish, *URGH* embodies chaos in an inventive and satisfying way.  
3. To Whom This May Concern, by Jill Scott - on her first album in 10 years, Jill Scott’s voice is both hopeful and lived-in. The warm instrumentals and production of this album work to center her vocals - particularly on tracks like “Offdaback” where she sings more conversationally over pensive piano chords. There are lots of flourishes over the record’s warm neo-soul palette, like the grandiose horns on “Be Great,” but none of them ever crowd Scott’s vocals and lyrics. Touching on themes of black joy, community and love, as well as reflection and aging (see the excellent jazzy single “Pressha”), *To Whom This May Concern* is a career-defining album and an early highlight of 2026.  
4. Laughter In Summer, by Beverly Glenn-Copeland - one of the most moving projects I’ve listened to in a long time. In 2023, music veteran Beverly Glenn-Copeland was diagnosed with dementia, a diagnosis that has left many desolate. After receiving this diagnosis, Glenn-Copeland decided to continue with his career, go on tour, and continue penning songs and reimagining older ones alongside his partner, Elizabeth Glenn-Copeland. The resulting album is a nourishing fusion of folk, new age, and neo-classical music, crafted with a community of Canadian musicians such as Helena Deland who were eager to uplift Glenn-Copeland and his artistry.   
5. The Apple Tree Under the Sea, by Hemlocke Springs - this was a long-awaited project for me! I have had my sights set on Hemlocke Springs since she debuted in 2023 with some of the best pop songs of the decade so far (particularly “girlfriend”), and her debut full-length record is every bit as playful, adventurous, and catchy as I hoped! But in the years she took to develop this project, she also honed in on some overarching concepts like temptation and unrequited love that make her an even more exciting voice in the pop landscape.  
6. Nothing’s About to Happen to Me, by Mitski - between this record and *The Land is Inhospitable,* I am realizing that Mitski’s voice and lyrics are such a natural fit for Americana that it’s incredible she didn’t experiment with it earlier. Lyrically, this new record is one of Mitski’s most ambitious as she puts herself in the shoes of a reclusive woman with an unkempt home. Within this character, she elegantly explores a host of themes like desperation (“I’ll Change for You”) and suicidal ideation (“Instead of Here”).  
7. The Pleasure is Yours, by Gena (Liv.e & Karriem Riggins) - a cosmic fusion between Riggins’ seasoned and extensive beatmaking and Liv.e’s fluid, unpredictable neo-soul. From the retro, neon-colored “This Is So Crazy” to the smoky “Lead It Up,” the duo achieve a lot of variety within their luxurious sound.  
8. MAN BITES DOG, by femtanyl - I put this on during my bus commute to work and was instantly swept into auditory euphoria like none other at 7 a.m. It’s a project so bitcrushed it’s like someone’s ramming a flail into both of your ears, and it completely rips.  
9. Marathon, by Maria BC - an uncannily beautiful gothic folk release that feels quietly aggressive in its textural depth, despite having a soft-spoken surface.  
10. Heaven Ain’t Sold, by Choker - after a 7 year hiatus, Choker has delivered a soft yet surprising record that warrants Frank Ocean comparisons more than anything else I’ve heard in years.

Great Albums: March 2026

1. Sentence Structure in the Country, by more eaze - Spellbinding from start to finish. Historically, more eaze has not been the easiest artist for me to digest, but “Sentence Structure in the Country” clicked for me immediately. The field recordings, ambient hisses, and instrumentation work to create the most interesting soundscapes I’ve heard in a while - especially when more eaze adds autotuned, tinny vocals to the mix. The sonic palette of the album is unnerving, but brightly colored (especially on tracks like Distance that have more of an acoustic makeup) - almost sounding like an inverse of sounds created by fellow ambient boundary-pusher aya, who also has been releasing music that I love. Definitely a treat, and endlessly replayable in terms of wanting to unpeel the layers!  
2. How to Make a Breakfast, by Land Whales - an unkempt, distorted rock record hailing from Cuba that mixes and regurgitates everything from Franz Kafka’s “The Trial” to Sonic Youth’s “Daydream Nation.” In the meantime, they balance chaotic passages with sugary chorus melodies, showing a particular talent for weaving their way through a variety of 90’s-indebted alt-rock styles while making something completely unique.  
3. In Another, by Masahiro Takahashi - a sunkissed ambient jazz album that feels perfect as the weather thaws. Its uniqueness lies in how Takahashi indulges in chamber music and surreal electronic touches, with “Queen West Butterfly” being a particular standout - if you don’t check out anything else from this list, that track is a must-listen!  
4. Trying Times, by James Blake - at this point in his career, it feels like a guarantee that anything James Blake puts out is likely worth a listen. However, “Trying Times” delivered even more heft than I was expecting. It feels like a very genuine and direct effort from James, particularly since his vocals are made more front-and-center here than they were in the past. This often means that he leans into more traditional pop structures, but his music still sounds as shimmery and alien as ever. I really enjoyed the musical crescendo in “I Had a Dream She Took My Hand.”  
5. Honora, by Flea - As someone who could not give less of a shit about the Red Hot Chili Peppers’ music, I didn’t really have expectations when I heard that the bassist of Flea was making a jazz record. However, I am so glad that I ended up giving this a chance, because it is a wonderful effort. On it, Flea enlists musicians from the L.A. jazz scene to help elevate the album’s sound, and there are even vocal features from Thom Yorke and Nick Cave (who I love very much).  
6. A Rush to Nowhere, by Arima Ederra - on this record, Ederra presents a chameleonic take on R&B that puts her right in line with other innovators of the genre, like Mk.gee and Dijon. With feather-light vocals and arrangements, she concocts a sound that feels absolutely heavenly - particularly on moments like the chorus of “Gemini Eyes.”  
7. Hard Hearted Woman, by Ora Cogan - a project that excitedly pushes country and Americana music into gorgeous, ghostly directions. Its fusion of Americana’s rustic sound with gothic and psychedelic touches make it both a nostalgic and refreshing listen.  
8. Hurts Like Hell, by Charlotte Cornfield - a folk/Americana record whose strength lies in the detailed portraits Charlotte creates of her characters as they experience life and love.  
9. Girlfriend, by Grace Ives - an album that strikes a rare balance between intimate bedroom pop and shimmering, nocturnal synthpop.  
10. Hinterlands, by Green-House - yet another serene, nature-inspired release from these ambient auteurs.  
11. Dying Is The Internet, by Simo Cell & Abdullah Miniawy - a daring combination of bombastic, thundering electronics and vocals inspired by Arabic poetry.  
12. Paradises, by Ladytron - amidst blissed-out synths, “Paradises” sees the electroclash group indulge more pop-centric impulses.  
13. I Guess U Had to Be There, by E L U C I D & Sebb Bash - a refreshingly direct hip-hop effort from this rapper-producer duo that navigates some truly eerie, minimalist beats.  
14. Future Present Past, by Irreversible Entanglements - a very complex effort led by vocalist Moor Mother that draws from across the black diaspora to make a refreshing, hopeful record.  
15. Companion, by Sluice - a straightforward folk rock release brought to life by earnest songwriting and interesting textures.

BONUS: Just a Few Excellent EPs - March 2026

1. BabyGoated, by Amy Gadiaga - there’s uplifting neo-soul, inspiring jazz, and experimental impulses in spades on this new Amy Gadiaga EP. “BabyGoated” cements her as not only a fantastic performer and arranger, but one of the most exciting recent voices in the London jazz scene.  
2. Is This All There Is? By Anna Calvi - there isn’t much I could write that would sell it more than the tracklist itself: there’s a propulsive, Iggy Pop-featured gothic banger (which is one of the best new songs I’ve heard of late), as well as covers of Bonnie “Prince” Billy and Kraftwerk that feature Perfume Genius and Laurie Anderson, respectively. Check it out!!!  
3. Swan Songs, by Model/Actriz - another Model/Actriz release, another opportunity for me to meatride them into the sunset on this website. Please check this out for more industrial-tinged ragers!  
4. The Weight, by Cashier - 6 propulsive, lo-fi pop punk numbers that cover the classic coming-of-age/lovelorn territory of emo and pop punk.  
5. I Sure Am Getting Sick of This Bowling Alley, by Mclusky - a brief, but solid effort that proves that Mclusky are still fantastic noise-rockers.  
6. It’s Glass, by Dutch Interior - after a particularly great studio album last year, Dutch Interior are already back with more downtempo, Americana-brushed folk tracks.  
7. (Un)planted, by midori jaeger - ethereal, cello-driven indie pop that finds flowy propulsion in naturalistic textures.  
8. BIG MAMA, by Flying Lotus - an unpredictable, wildly fun EP that sees Flying Lotus do a full 180 into hyperactive, cartoonish electronic music.  
9. Temesgen, by ZENA - a London based Ethio-jazz effort that combines the genre’s tonality with global influences from Latin and soul music.  
10. 97, by Ana Tijoux & DJ Dacel - a Spanish-language conscious hip hop record that leans into a jazzy, old school sound.
